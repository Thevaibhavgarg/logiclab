# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

LogicLab aims to be a welcoming place to learn, build, and contribute. Everyone
should be able to participate respectfully, regardless of experience, age,
disability, appearance, gender identity or expression, nationality, ethnicity,
religion, or sexual orientation.

## Expected behavior

- Treat others with respect and patience, especially newcomers.
- Give constructive feedback about ideas and code, not personal attacks.
- Welcome different perspectives and ask questions in good faith.
- Respect personal boundaries and privacy.
- Take responsibility for mistakes and help resolve disagreements calmly.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, discrimination, or insulting and demeaning language
- Unwanted sexual attention or sexualized comments and imagery
- Publishing another person's private information without permission
- Repeatedly disrupting discussions or targeting people across project spaces
- Retaliation against someone who raises a concern or participates in a review

## Scope

This code applies to project issues, pull requests, reviews, and other
project-managed community spaces. It also applies when someone explicitly
represents LogicLab in public.

## Raising concerns

For a non-sensitive concern, flag the relevant interaction to the repository
maintainer, [@Thevaibhavgarg](https://github.com/Thevaibhavgarg), in the project
space where it occurred. Share only the information needed to understand the
concern.

A dedicated private reporting channel is not currently published. Do not post
sensitive reports, personal information, or private evidence in public issues
or comments. You can use GitHub's reporting tools to report abusive content or
users to GitHub; those reports are handled by GitHub, not the project maintainer.

## Enforcement

The maintainer is responsible for applying this code fairly and considering the
context and impact of reported behavior. Responses may include asking for a
behavior change, removing inappropriate content, limiting participation, or
blocking a participant from the repository for serious or repeated violations.

Participants must respect moderation decisions and must not retaliate against
others. Questions about a decision should remain respectful and must not expose
private information.
